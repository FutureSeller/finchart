A `<ChartSeries>` or `<ChartPane>` switched on by its own component's state now takes its JSX place instead of landing at the back until its pane or the container rendered again; the pane or container renders once more to place it, and the pane's list never shows the series at the back in between. A `plot.setPaneOrder` you made now holds until a `<ChartPane>` mounts or moves past another in the JSX: a series appearing between two panes, or a pane going, no longer restacks the panes. A main pane whose `<ChartPane>` went goes back to the top at the next restack, and goes to the next `<ChartPane>` to arrive — mounted, or shown again by `<Activity>`.

`<ChartPane>` now declares itself to its container, which builds, ranks and places a pane arriving before the browser paints, on React 18 and 19 and for sync, default and transition updates alike — before, a mounting pane could be drawn empty at the bottom of the stack for a frame (on React 18 for any sync update); a pane whose content suspends on mount still sits empty until it resolves inside a Suspense boundary of its own within the chart; with the nearest boundary outside the chart, that boundary shows its fallback instead, even for a transition. A pane still leaves in the same passive flush as its `<ChartPane>` and the series inside it. What follows from that:

- A Suspense boundary hiding a `<ChartPane>` — one pane or the whole chart — leaves its pane on the chart as it is: its series, the user's divider drag and axis-drag range, the components inside it with their state, and a `plot.setPaneOrder` you made. Showing it applies any prop that changed meanwhile, announcing nothing unless one did. While hidden it stays right after the pane it followed, whatever is added or removed around it, so a pane inserted between that one and it meanwhile sits after it until it shows again; the reveal then restacks the panes to JSX order. A component inside a pane may suspend inside the pane's own boundary. In development, React 19's StrictMode replays effects on a Suspense reveal, which rebuilds a pane hidden on its own, the components inside it starting over, and gives a chart hidden whole a new `Plot`; React 18 and production keep them. A pane deleted while hidden is gone when the boundary shows again, and a main pane it held is released with its scale restored.
- An `<Activity>` hiding one `<ChartPane>` takes its pane off the chart, with any `<ChartPane>` nested in it, and showing it builds the pane afresh from its current props in its JSX place, with the components inside it starting over, restacking the declared panes to JSX order. An `<Activity>` hiding the whole chart destroys it and builds a new one on showing.
- Unmounting the chart, or remounting it under a new `key`, leaves the panes to the chart's own teardown instead of calling `removePane` on the destroyed chart for each one; no `panesChange` rings either way.
- Under StrictMode a pane mounted with the chart is built once, on React 18 as on 19; before, the replay acquired it twice and called a `yScale` factory once more. A pane added later by a render of the container itself is still built, taken off and built again by the replay, as before.

Types: `ChartApi` and `SeriesCollector` are no longer exported. They were the wrapper's channel between the container and its children, no public hook handed either out, and this change rewrites both.
