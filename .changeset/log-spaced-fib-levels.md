A Fibonacci retracement or extension can space its levels in log price: `levelSpacing: "log"` puts level `l` at `b·(a/b)^l` (an extension at `c·(b/a)^l`) instead of evenly in price, which is what looks even on a log axis — price-linear levels bunch toward one end there. The field belongs to the drawing and does not look at the scale, so a saved drawing draws its lines at the same prices on any axis; on a linear axis it is the log-spaced levels that bunch. It is set through `add` and `handle.update` (`undefined` goes back), survives save and load without a format bump, and undo and redo restore it. A level log spacing cannot define — an anchor price that is not positive, or a result outside the doubles — is not drawn and cannot be grabbed; the anchors' own levels (a retracement's 0% and 100%, an extension's 0%) stay defined, so they are drawn wherever the level list has them. Dragging a log-spaced drawing by its body moves its prices by a common factor, so the grabbed level stays under the cursor; a move log price cannot express is not applied. That holds when every anchor's price and the cursor's price at the grab are positive — otherwise the whole gesture moves by a common amount, as before. A saved `levelSpacing` this build does not know is dropped on load like an unknown field, and the rest of the ledger loads. `drawingTools({ defaults: { fib, fibExtension } })` gives hand-drawn Fibonaccis their `levels` and `levelSpacing` from birth; `add` is unaffected. A Fibonacci level whose price is not finite is no longer emitted as a line or a label.
